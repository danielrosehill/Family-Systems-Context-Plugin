# Glossary: the user's words, and what they map to

People who come out of abusive families often find their first language for it in the
informal recovery literature: forums, books and videos about narcissistic abuse. Those
words are precise to the people who use them. A therapist may use different ones. This
glossary sits between the two.

**For the agent:**

- Never discourage, correct or replace the user's terms. Use them back in conversation.
- Use this table to understand what the user means. When a term is ambiguous, ask once
  what they mean by it and record the answer in `vocabulary.md` at the workspace root,
  which overrides this file for this user.
- In actor files and the overview, record the user's term **and** its mapping, for
  example: `Recruited the aunts to pressure the user ("flying monkeys"; coalition,
  Minuchin, Tier B)`. The intake pack prints both, so a clinician reads it in their terms.
- Tiers are explained in [`frameworks.md`](frameworks.md). A Tier C mapping is not a
  weaker claim about what happened; it means the *term* comes from practice or popular
  literature rather than measured research.

## People and roles

| Term | What the user usually means | Research mapping | Record as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Narcissist**, narcissistic parent | A person centred on their own image and needs, entitled, exploitative, without empathy for the user, punishing when challenged | The user's characterisation, recorded as theirs. Trait research distinguishes **grandiose** and **vulnerable** narcissism (Miller, J. D., et al. (2011). *Journal of Personality, 79*(5), 1013–1042; Tier A as traits). Narcissistic personality disorder is a clinical diagnosis (DSM-5-TR) and is recorded only if a clinician made it | The user's term, plus the specific behaviours |
| **Covert narcissist** | The same pattern, hidden behind fragility, martyrdom, victimhood or quiet resentment | Vulnerable narcissism (Tier A as a trait construct) | User's term + behaviours |
| **Flying monkeys** | People recruited by the person causing harm to pressure, monitor, guilt or carry messages to the user, knowingly or not | **Coalitions** (Minuchin, B); **interlocking triangles** (Bowen, B); use of third parties in **coercive control** (Stark, B); **relational aggression** when it targets reputation (Tier A in child and adolescent research: Crick, N. R., & Grotpeter, J. K. (1995). *Child Development, 66*(3), 710–722) | Who, recruited by whom, what they did, whether they seemed to know |
| **Enabler** | A family member who protects, excuses or covers for the person causing harm | Wegscheider-Cruse role (C); in structural terms, a coalition with the person causing harm or a failure of the parental subsystem to protect (B) | User's term + what they did and did not do |
| **Golden child** | The favoured child, held up as proof the family is fine | Parental **differential treatment** (studied in developmental research, e.g. Jensen, A. C., Whiteman, S. D., Fingerman, K. L., & Birditt, K. S. (2013). *Journal of Marriage and Family, 75*(2), 438–452; A); family hero (Wegscheider-Cruse, C) | User's term + the favouring behaviours |
| **Scapegoat**, black sheep | The child blamed for the family's problems | Scapegoating (Vogel & Bell, B); family projection process (Bowen, B); detouring (Minuchin, B); differential treatment (A) | User's term + instances of blame |
| **Lost child**, invisible child | The child no one noticed, who disappeared to stay safe | Wegscheider-Cruse role (C); disengaged boundaries (B); emotional neglect (an ACE category, A) | User's term + what was missing |
| **Parentified child** | A child made to look after a parent, siblings or the household, or to be a parent's confidant | Parentification, instrumental or emotional (Jurkovic; Hooper et al. 2011; A/B) | Which kind, from what age, for whom |
| **Emotional incest**, covert incest, surrogate spouse | A parent using a child as a partner substitute for emotional intimacy, without sexual contact | **Emotional parentification** and **cross-generational coalition** (B); the term "covert incest" is from popular clinical writing (Adams, K. M. (1991). *Silently Seduced*; C). Distinct from sexual abuse, which is recorded as sexual abuse | User's term + the specific demands and confidences |
| **Fleas** | Habits the user picked up from an abusive parent and dislikes in themselves | Survival adaptations, learned behaviour (C as a term) | In `self.md`, in the user's words, as adaptations, not faults |

## Tactics

| Term | What the user usually means | Research mapping | Record as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gaslighting** | Being told their memory, perception or sanity is wrong, until they doubt themselves | Gaslighting (Sweet, 2019; B) | The claim made, and what the user's own record shows |
| **DARVO** | Raising a harm and being met with denial, attack, and the other person casting themselves as the victim | DARVO (Freyd; Harsey et al. 2017; A) | Each step, in order |
| **Hoovering** | Attempts to pull the user back into contact or compliance after they pulled away: sudden warmth, crises, guilt, illness, gifts | Pressure on a member who changes position to change back, a system reaction described in Bowen theory (B); intermittent reinforcement (A as a learning principle) | Each attempt: date, channel, content, what preceded it |
| **Love bombing** | Overwhelming warmth or generosity used to win back or gain control | Idealisation phase in cyclical abuse descriptions (C) | Dates and what followed |
| **Trauma bond** | A strong attachment to the person causing harm, held in place by alternating harm and relief | **Traumatic bonding** (Dutton, D. G., & Painter, S. L. (1981). *Victimology, 6*, 139–155; B) | In `self.md`, in the user's words |
| **Triangulation** (popular sense) | Being played off against a sibling or relative, compared, or reached through a third person instead of directly | Overlaps with **triangles** (Bowen, B) and **triangulation** (Minuchin: a child pressed to side with one parent; B). The clinical terms are narrower; record which shape it is | Who, about whom, through whom |
| **Smear campaign** | Being discredited to relatives, friends or community, often in advance | Relational aggression (A in youth research); reputational tactics within coercive control (B) | What was said, to whom, how the user learned of it |
| **Silent treatment** | Being punished by withdrawal of speech or acknowledgement | **Ostracism** (Williams, K. D. (2007). *Annual Review of Psychology, 58*, 425–452; A) | Duration, trigger, how it ended |
| **Stonewalling** | Shutting down and refusing to engage when something is raised | Stonewalling (Gottman, couples research; A in that context) | Instances |
| **Word salad** | Circular, contradictory arguing that leaves the user confused and exhausted | Irrelevant stance (Satir, C); deflection as part of DARVO (A) | A verbatim example where possible |
| **Projection** | The other person accusing the user of exactly what they themselves are doing | Projection as a defence mechanism (psychodynamic, B) | The accusation and the matching behaviour |
| **Future faking** | Promises of change or reward that never arrive | Broken-promise pattern (C) | Promise, date, outcome |
| **Devalue and discard** | Being idealised, then torn down, then dropped | Idealisation and devaluation (psychodynamic, B/C) | The cycle, with dates |
| **Narcissistic rage** | Explosive anger when the person's image or control is challenged | Narcissistic rage (Kohut, H. (1972). *The Psychoanalytic Study of the Child, 27*, 360–400; B/C) | Trigger, behaviour, aftermath |
| **Narcissistic supply** | Attention, admiration or service the person extracts from others | Popular term (C); in contextual therapy terms, an imbalanced ledger (B/C) | What was extracted, from whom |
| **Boundary stomping** | Ignoring or punishing limits the user sets | Boundary violation; enmeshed boundaries (Minuchin, B) | The limit, the response |
| **Rug-sweeping** | Harm being ignored or hushed so the family image holds | Family rules and secrets ("don't talk", B); denial | The event and who hushed it |
| **Cycle of abuse** | Tension, incident, reconciliation, calm, repeating | Walker's cycle (Walker, L. E. (1979). *The Battered Woman*; C, limited empirical support) | The cycles observed, with dates |

## The user's strategies and states

| Term | What the user usually means | Research mapping | Record as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **No contact**, low contact, very low contact | The user's decision about contact | Contact status. The user's decision, recorded as such and not questioned | `contact:` in the actor file, plus the date and reason in History |
| **Grey rock** | Being deliberately dull and unreactive to give the person nothing to use | A protective strategy (C as a term) | In `self.md`, as a strategy |
| **Walking on eggshells** | Constant alertness to a family member's mood | Chronic anxiety in the system (Bowen, B); hypervigilance (trauma literature, B) | In `self.md` and in the overview's family rules |
| **Enmeshment** | No privacy or separateness; feelings and decisions treated as family property | Enmeshed boundaries (Minuchin, B); low differentiation (Bowen, B) | Instances |
| **Emotional abuse** | Sustained belittling, threats, humiliation, rejection | Psychological maltreatment; emotional abuse is an ACE category (A) | Instances |

## Adding terms

When the user uses a term that is not here, ask what they mean by it, then add it to the
workspace's `vocabulary.md` with their definition and a mapping. Do not add it to this file
inside a workspace; this file comes from the template.
